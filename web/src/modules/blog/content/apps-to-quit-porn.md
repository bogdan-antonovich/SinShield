If you search for an app to quit porn, you will find products that do completely different jobs under the same promise. One blocks websites, another counts the time since your last viewing session, another sends activity to someone you trust, and another provides lessons or a peer group; choosing between them begins with deciding what you need the app to change.

No app can cure an addiction, guarantee recovery, or remove every route to explicit content. A useful app can add friction, preserve a record, create an honest conversation, teach a skill, or connect you with support, and those smaller jobs can matter when they are part of a plan you are willing to keep using.

> [!NOTE] The short answer
> Choose a blocker when immediate access is the problem, a habit tracker when you need to see patterns, an accountability app when another person’s involvement helps, and a support program when you want structured lessons or peers; combine categories only when each one has a clear job.

## Four kinds of quit-porn apps

Search results mix these categories because they serve the same broad goal, but their limits are different enough that the label “quit porn app” does not tell you what will happen after installation.

| Category | Useful for | Cannot do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blocker | Adding friction and limiting access | Treat underlying behavioral or mental-health problems |
| Habit tracker | Recording progress, triggers, and patterns | Prevent access |
| Accountability app | Sharing defined activity with another person | Guarantee honesty or prevent every workaround |
| Support app | Education, exercises, peer support, or coaching | Replace professional care when it is needed |

Some products cross categories. Covenant Eyes combines blocking and accountability, while Fortify combines tracking, lessons, and community features; that does not make either a universal solution, because a wide feature list still leaves the question of which mechanism will help you in the difficult moment you actually face.

## Start with the job you need done

Before comparing brands, write one sentence about the last two or three times the behavior happened. If the sequence began by opening a familiar site, access is the first problem; if it began after a predictable argument or sleepless night, understanding and planning for the trigger may matter more than another domain list.

- **Choose blocking** when the path is fast, familiar, and mostly digital, especially when one extra step would give you time to leave the situation.
- **Choose tracking** when days blur together or you keep making the same promise without noticing the time, place, emotion, and device that precede the behavior.
- **Choose accountability** when secrecy is keeping the loop intact and you are willing to let a specific, trustworthy person see defined activity and follow up.
- **Choose structured support** when you want exercises, education, regular check-ins, or a peer group rather than only a technical barrier.
- **Consider professional care** when the behavior feels persistently out of control, is escalating, or is seriously affecting work, relationships, finances, sleep, safety, or mental health.

The categories can work together. A blocker can protect the first ten seconds, a tracker can reveal that Sunday night is a recurring weak point, and a human conversation can help you change what happens before the phone is even in your hand.

## Blocker apps: best for reducing immediate access

A porn blocker interrupts websites, searches, apps, or visible content so that an urge does not have a completely open path. It changes the environment rather than the underlying reason you wanted the content, which is why it works best as part of a wider plan.

### CleanBrowsing: free adult-domain filtering

[CleanBrowsing](https://cleanbrowsing.org/filters) provides free DNS filters that can be configured on an Android phone or home router. **Best for:** a free website layer across browsers or several devices on a home network.

**Cannot do:** inspect individual images, posts, or messages inside an allowed domain.

## Habit trackers: best for seeing patterns and progress

A tracker does not know what is open on your screen and cannot stop a website from loading. Its value is continuity: it preserves the reasons you chose to change, records slips without erasing the history around them, and turns “this keeps happening” into a pattern you can examine.

### Quitzilla: a private general-purpose habit tracker

[Quitzilla](https://www.quitzilla.com/) is not specific to pornography and does not diagnose or treat addiction. Its free plan tracks up to two habits and includes a sobriety counter, statistics, motivational quotes, reasons, goals, rewards, and relapse history, while premium adds unlimited habits, customization, and home-screen widgets.

Quitzilla says habit and progress data stay locally on the device rather than being uploaded to its servers. That makes it suitable when you want a private record, although a timer can become too dominant if one slip makes you treat every earlier improvement as lost.

**Best for:** recording streaks, slips, reasons, and trends without monitoring browsing activity.

**Cannot do:** block a site, detect explicit content, contact another person, or explain why a pattern keeps returning.

Use a tracker to record actions as well as abstinence. Note whether you moved the phone out of the bedroom, used your planned response, contacted someone, or closed an unexpected trigger quickly, because those behaviors show which parts of the system are getting stronger even when the streak is imperfect.

## Accountability apps: best when another person should know

Accountability software creates a defined flow of information to someone else. That may be a report, blurred screenshot, uninstall notification, or check-in, and the mechanism works only when both people understand what is collected, consent to the arrangement, and agree on what the other person should do with the information.

### Covenant Eyes: blocking plus a human Ally

[Victory by Covenant Eyes](https://www.covenanteyes.com/victory/) combines explicit-domain blocking with screen accountability and an Ally relationship across supported versions of Android, iOS, Windows, and macOS. On Android, Windows, and Mac it can take screenshots, process them for concerning content, and send accountability information to the chosen Ally; the service says screenshots are blurred, encrypted, and deleted after 30 days.

Current website pricing is $18 per month, $198 per year, or $950 for a lifetime membership, with a trial or money-back terms that depend on how you subscribe. The central tradeoff is clear: another person receives information because that visibility is the product’s main source of accountability, so it is a poor fit when private, on-device protection is non-negotiable.

**Best for:** someone who wants a trusted person involved across several device types and is comfortable with activity leaving the device.

**Cannot do:** guarantee that the person tells the whole story, prevent every workaround, or make an inattentive or punitive Ally helpful.

## Support and program apps: best for structure and connection

Support apps add lessons, exercises, daily check-ins, journaling, coaching, or peer groups. They can give the week a structure and reduce isolation, but their content and communities should be treated as support rather than proof that the app can cure a condition or replace a licensed clinician.

### Fortify: self-guided lessons, tracking, and community

[Fortify on Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.ftnd.fortify) offers a personalized assessment, videos, challenges, goal tracking, analytics, an SOS function, and community or coaching features. The current Android listing contains ads and in-app purchases, while some learning and community features have moved behind paid access over time, so check the live plan before assuming a specific module is free.

Fortify is broader than a streak counter because it asks the user to work through material and reflect on patterns. Its Play disclosure also says the app may collect location, personal information, and other data and may share personal information and device identifiers, which matters when the subject itself is sensitive.

**Best for:** someone who wants guided exercises and a recovery-oriented program rather than only blocking.

**Cannot do:** ensure that exercises are completed, replace individualized professional care, or guarantee that a community is the right fit for every reader.

### Relay: a paid peer-support program

[Relay](https://www.joinrelay.app/) combines daily check-ins, a private support group, therapist-created lessons, journaling, trigger work, and progress tracking. Its current offer is $149 per year with a seven-day trial and a stated 30-day guarantee, making it a program subscription rather than a permanently free quit-porn app.

Relay’s main value proposition is connection with other people following a structured path. That can be useful when isolation is central to the pattern, but a peer group is not therapy, group fit matters, and sharing sensitive information requires reading the privacy terms and deciding what you are comfortable placing in the service.

**Best for:** someone who wants a recurring peer group and guided program in one app.

**Cannot do:** replace emergency help, diagnose a disorder, provide the same care as an individual licensed clinician, or make group participation automatically safe and useful.

## A practical way to combine apps

More apps are not automatically better. Every permission prompt, notification, subscription, and daily check-in adds maintenance, so start with the smallest combination that covers two different failure points.

### If access happens too quickly

Use one blocker on every device you actually use, remove saved material and bookmarks, and write a ten-minute response for the moment a block appears. The app closes the shortest route; the response tells you where to go next instead of leaving you in the same position searching for a workaround.

### If you lose track of the pattern

Use a tracker for two weeks and record time, place, device, emotion, first digital action, and what you tried instead. Review it once a week, then change one recurring condition such as charging the phone outside the bedroom or leaving a particular app signed out.

### If secrecy keeps the loop intact

Choose one person and make the agreement specific: what they will see, how often they will check in, what counts as a concern, and how they should respond. Accountability should create a path back to the plan, not surveillance, humiliation, or a contest over who can control the device.

### If you need skills or a community

Try one structured program long enough to judge whether you actually complete its exercises and feel safer rather than more ashamed. If the program’s claims, culture, privacy model, or group dynamics do not fit, leaving it is a decision about fit rather than proof that support itself cannot help.

> [!NOTE] A simple starter system
> One blocker for the fastest route, one written record of triggers and responses, and one person or professional resource you can contact is usually more usable than five overlapping apps.

## When an app is not enough

You do not need a diagnosis before changing a behavior that no longer fits your life. Professional support becomes especially worth considering, however, when you repeatedly lose control despite serious attempts to stop, the behavior is escalating, or it is interfering with relationships, work, school, sleep, finances, sexual functioning, or safety.

A licensed mental-health professional familiar with compulsive sexual behavior or problematic pornography use can help assess the wider picture, including anxiety, depression, trauma, relationship conflict, substance use, or distress driven mainly by a conflict with personal values. An app can support that work, but it should not tell you that one streak, score, or “rewiring” timeline proves you are cured.

If you might harm yourself or someone else, or if the content involves exploitation or a minor, do not wait for an app to solve the situation. Contact local emergency services, a crisis service, or the appropriate safeguarding authority now.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best app to quit porn?

There is no best app for every person because blockers, trackers, accountability tools, and support programs solve different problems. Choose the category that matches the moment your current plan fails, then compare privacy, platform coverage, cost, and how easily you can keep using it.

### Can an app make me stop watching porn?

No app can make or guarantee that change. It can block a route, record a pattern, alert another person, or guide an exercise, while your environment, prepared responses, relationships, and professional support still determine much of what happens around it.

### Are porn blockers and quit-porn apps the same thing?

No. A porn blocker limits access or visibility, while a broader quit-porn app may only track progress, provide education, connect you with peers, or send reports to another person.

### Should I use a streak tracker?

A streak can provide a clear milestone, but it should not turn one slip into the belief that all progress disappeared. Keep the history, record what preceded the slip, and track protective actions so the number helps you learn rather than judge yourself.

### Do I need an accountability partner?

Not everyone does. Human accountability may help when secrecy is central and you have a trustworthy person who can respond constructively, while private blocking or self-tracking may be a better starting point when monitoring would create risk, shame, or avoidance.

### Can a quit-porn app replace therapy?

No. Apps can provide useful tools and support, but they cannot perform a full clinical assessment, manage every co-occurring condition, or replace individualized care when the behavior causes significant distress, impairment, or danger.
