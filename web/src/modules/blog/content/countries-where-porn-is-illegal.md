If you searched for a list of countries where porn is illegal, you probably want a practical answer before traveling, moving abroad, or opening material that is ordinary at home but risky somewhere else. The important answer is that several countries impose broad restrictions on pornography, including Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, Indonesia, and China, but a simple banned-or-legal list can still mislead you because importing a file, possessing it, streaming it, producing it, and sending it to another person may all be treated differently.

Some governments prohibit pornographic material at the border, some criminalize production or distribution without expressly criminalizing private viewing, and some block adult websites even though an adult viewer is not the target of the law. In countries such as the United Kingdom, Australia, and the United States, lawful adult content is generally available but subject to age, obscenity, production, or platform rules, so a blocked page does not by itself mean that viewing pornography is a crime.

> [!NOTE] The short answer
> There is no dependable one-line list of “porn-banned countries.” Treat Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Indonesia, and China as clear examples of highly restrictive environments, but check the exact rule for access, possession, import, and sharing before relying on any country label.

This article provides general information, not legal advice. Laws and enforcement can change quickly, translations may lose important detail, and local authorities or a lawyer qualified in the relevant country are the right sources for a decision that could expose you to a penalty.

## What does “porn is illegal” actually mean?

The word *ban* is often used for several different legal controls, and collapsing them into one column is where many online country lists go wrong. Before deciding what a rule means for you, separate the conduct being regulated.

- **Private viewing** means opening material for yourself without showing, sending, or selling it to anyone else. A law against distribution does not automatically make this conduct illegal, although another possession or cybercrime rule may still apply.

- **Possession or storage** concerns files saved on a phone, laptop, cloud account, drive, or physical medium. This can matter at a border even when you did not intend to publish anything.

- **Import and export** concern bringing material into or out of a country, including on storage media and, depending on local law, through electronic transmission. Customs rules can be stricter than the rule applied to viewing inside the country.

- **Production and distribution** cover making, uploading, selling, sending, broadcasting, or publicly displaying material. These are the activities most consistently addressed by adult-content and obscenity laws.

- **Website blocking** is an access-control measure used by governments or internet providers. It proves that access is restricted, but it does not tell you by itself whether an individual viewer commits an offence.

- **Age assurance and classification** limit who may access material and how a service may provide it. These rules regulate lawful adult content rather than creating a blanket ban for adults.

Content involving the sexual abuse or exploitation of a child is a separate and much more serious category. It is better described as child sexual abuse material, or CSAM, because it records abuse rather than consensual adult pornography, and you should never assume that a rule permitting some adult content applies to it.

## Countries with broad pornography restrictions

The examples below are not a complete world list. They are countries for which current legislation or official travel and customs guidance establishes a broad restriction, while the final column marks the boundary of what that source alone can prove.

| Country | What a current official source establishes | What you should not assume |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Saudi Arabia | Saudi customs lists storage media containing material contrary to public morals among prohibited passenger goods, and official UK travel guidance says pornographic material is illegal to import. | An import rule does not provide a complete answer about every form of private online viewing. |
| United Arab Emirates | Current travel guidance says bringing pornography into the UAE is illegal and warns that videos, books, and magazines may be examined or censored. | The customs rule alone does not define the penalty for every streamed image or link. |
| Indonesia | Law No. 44 of 2008 remains in force and establishes broad prohibitions involving the production, reproduction, distribution, broadcasting, import, export, offering, trade, rental, and provision of pornography. | Do not reduce a detailed statute, its exceptions, and later amendments to a single claim that every private view is prosecuted. |
| China | Articles 363 to 367 of the Criminal Law punish producing, duplicating, publishing, selling, or disseminating pornographic material, with additional rules for broadcasting and dissemination to minors. | Those provisions do not say that passive private viewing is the same offence as dissemination. |

For Saudi Arabia, the safest travel practice is not to carry explicit downloads or storage media into the country. The [Saudi customs handbook](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/RulesRegulations/Taxes/Pages/customs-individual/Travel-pages/disclosure-amounts.aspx) says prohibited items can expose a traveler to legal responsibility, imprisonment, and a fine, while the [current entry guidance](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/saudi-arabia/entry-requirements) notes that electronic devices and personal videos may be examined.

The UAE should be approached with the same care because the [current customs guidance for travelers](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/entry-requirements) expressly says that bringing pornography into the country is illegal. If you need an exact answer about cached files, cloud storage, a messaging app, or a work device, ask the relevant embassy or a local lawyer rather than trying to stretch the word *bring* into a legal interpretation of your own.

Indonesia's [Pornography Law, Law No. 44 of 2008](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/39740/uu-no-44-tahun-2008), is a useful example of why summaries need care: it is broad and restrictive, but it contains definitions and provisions that must be read together, and its official record notes that part of the law was affected by the 2023 Criminal Code. The sound conclusion is that Indonesia has extensive legal controls over pornography, not that an SEO table can predict how every private act will be classified or enforced.

China also illustrates the difference between access and criminal conduct. Adult sites are heavily filtered, while [Articles 363 and 364 of the Criminal Law](https://en.spp.gov.cn/2020-12/26/c_948417_13.htm) expressly address commercial production and dissemination as well as serious non-commercial dissemination; describing those provisions as a ban on every private view would go beyond the text cited.

Other countries are frequently placed on broad-ban lists, including Afghanistan, Bahrain, Bangladesh, Brunei, Iran, Kuwait, Malaysia, the Maldives, Oman, Pakistan, and Qatar. Their laws and enforcement may indeed be highly restrictive, but you should verify the current statute and official travel advice for the particular country instead of treating inclusion in an unsourced list as a complete legal opinion.

## Countries where adult porn is regulated rather than completely banned

Many countries allow adults to access at least some pornographic material while prohibiting particular content or controlling how it reaches them. That is not an unregulated environment; it is a different legal model.

### United Kingdom

Adult pornography is not subject to a blanket ban in the UK, but unlawful content remains prohibited and online services that provide pornography must prevent children from encountering it. Ofcom says [highly effective age checks have been required since July 2025](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/online-age-checks-must-be-in-force-from-tomorrow), which means a service may ask an adult to prove or estimate their age even though the underlying adult content is lawful.

### Australia

Australia regulates pornography through classification, online-safety, and age-restriction rules rather than making all adult access a crime. The eSafety Commissioner states that adults retain the right to access lawful content, while [mandatory Age-Restricted Material Codes](https://www.esafety.gov.au/industry/codes/faq-access-to-online-porn-and-other-adult-content) require covered services to protect children and apply appropriate age checks.

### United States

Consensual adult pornography is not simply interchangeable with legally obscene material in the United States. The Department of Justice explains that federal law targets the distribution, transport, sale, import, and production-for-distribution of obscenity, while [private possession of obscene matter is generally not criminalized under federal law](https://www.justice.gov/criminal/criminal-ceos/obscenity); state law and the facts of a particular case can still matter.

### India, Japan, and South Korea

These countries are also regularly mislabeled by all-or-nothing lists because their laws clearly restrict transmission or public distribution without turning that wording into a universal private-viewing offence. India's [Information Technology Act, section 67](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/13116/1/it_act_2000_updated.pdf) addresses publishing or transmitting obscene material electronically, Japan's [Penal Code article 175](https://www.japaneselawtranslation.go.jp/ja/laws/view/3581) addresses distribution and public display, and South Korea's [Criminal Act article 243](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/eng/engLsInfoR.do?lsiSeq=253323) addresses distributing, selling, lending, or openly displaying obscene material.

That does not make every kind of content lawful in those countries, and it does not guarantee that every adult site will be accessible. It means the prohibited act in the cited provision matters, so “adult sites are blocked” and “a private adult viewer commits a crime” should not be presented as identical claims.

## Rules that apply almost everywhere

Even where consensual adult pornography is legal, the content and the conduct around it can cross firm legal lines. Exact definitions and penalties vary, but several categories require particular care.

- **CSAM is abuse material, not ordinary adult content.** Never download, save, forward, or investigate suspected material yourself; leave the page and use the reporting channel provided by the relevant national authority or platform.

- **Non-consensual intimate imagery may be criminal.** Sharing a private image without permission, including a manipulated sexual image in many jurisdictions, is legally and ethically different from consensually produced adult media.

- **Public display and sending can change the legal analysis.** Material privately held by an adult may become an offence when displayed in public, sent without consent, sold, or made available to a minor.

- **Travel creates an import question.** A saved file that was lawful where you downloaded it may be prohibited when the device crosses a border, and automatic downloads, message attachments, or offline folders can make possession less obvious to you without making it irrelevant to customs.

## How to check the law before you travel

Do not begin with a search snippet that says only “legal” or “illegal.” Use a short verification process that identifies both the source and the conduct you need to understand.

### 1. Check the destination's official customs guidance

Look for prohibited and restricted goods, media, publications, electronic devices, and storage media. Customs pages are especially useful for answering whether material can cross the border, which is often the immediate concern for a traveler.

### 2. Read current government travel advice

Use your own government's destination page as a second source, then check the destination government's portal or embassy. Read the update date and the exact verb used: *import*, *possess*, *publish*, *transmit*, and *view* do not mean the same thing.

### 3. Ask about your specific situation

If the answer could affect your liberty, visa, employment, or device search, contact the destination's embassy or a qualified local lawyer. Describe the conduct precisely without sending explicit material: for example, ask about stored files on a personal phone, not merely whether “porn is banned.”

### 4. Remove material before crossing a strict border

Where official guidance identifies pornography as prohibited, the low-risk choice is not to travel with it. Review offline folders, message downloads, browser downloads, cloud files marked for offline access, and removable storage before departure, while remembering that deletion and device-search law are separate technical and legal questions.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which countries have completely banned porn?

Several countries maintain very broad restrictions, but “completely banned” is rarely precise enough to describe every act and exception. Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Indonesia, and China are clear high-restriction examples, while any longer list should distinguish viewing, possession, import, production, distribution, and network blocking country by country.

### Can you be arrested for watching porn?

Yes, legal risk can exist in restrictive jurisdictions, particularly where possession, import, or electronic access falls within a local offence. Enforcement differs, so do not rely on a claim that authorities “usually” focus only on producers or distributors.

### Is porn legal in the United States?

Some consensual adult pornography is lawful, but CSAM is illegal and obscenity is not protected by the First Amendment. Federal law regulates activities such as distributing and transporting obscene material, while state laws can add other restrictions.

### Is porn legal in the United Kingdom and Australia?

Lawful adult pornography is generally available in both countries, but access is regulated and some content is illegal. Services face age-assurance and child-safety duties, which is regulation rather than a blanket adult ban.

### Does a blocked porn site mean watching porn is illegal?

No. A site may be blocked because of nationwide censorship, a court order, an age-assurance rule, a platform decision, or the site's own withdrawal from a market, and none of those facts alone identifies the offence that applies to an individual viewer.

### Will a VPN make access legal?

No. A VPN may change the route your traffic takes, but it does not change the law that applies where you are, and bypassing a government or service restriction may create additional risk under local rules.
