The best Covenant Eyes alternative depends on what you are trying to replace. Covenant Eyes, now delivered through Victory and Victory Shield, combines website filtering with activity monitoring and a human Ally; a private blocker, a real-time content filter, and Apple's or Google's built-in controls each replace only part of that job.

Decide first whether another person seeing activity is essential, acceptable, or exactly what you want to avoid. That choice separates accountability software from private protection more clearly than a list of features.

> [!NOTE] Disclosure
> SinShield is our product. We compare it here because it is one private Android alternative, but it does not reproduce Covenant Eyes' cross-platform coverage, screenshot reporting, Ally relationship, courses, or community.

## What Covenant Eyes currently provides

Covenant Eyes' [support page](https://support.covenanteyes.com/hc/en-us/articles/11980844648987-What-devices-does-Victory-Shield-work-on) describes Victory Shield as supporting Android 11+, iOS 15.1+, macOS 11+, and Windows 10+, while the current US App Store listing requires a newer iOS version, so iPhone owners should verify compatibility at install. On Android, Mac, and Windows it can take screen captures for accountability; on iPhone, screenshot accountability is limited to Safari, while device-wide domain monitoring and filtering use a VPN-like process.

Its monitoring system takes periodic screenshots, analyzes them, modifies and blurs them on the device, and transfers them so questionable activity and selected images can be reviewed by an Ally. Covenant Eyes' [monitoring documentation](https://support.covenanteyes.com/hc/en-us/articles/11976472404763-How-does-monitoring-work) says transferred images are stored in its encrypted database and not kept in the Victory app beyond the stated retention period.

As of September 24, 2026, Covenant Eyes lists [website sign-up prices](https://support.covenanteyes.com/hc/en-us/articles/11436400602395-On-how-many-devices-can-I-install-Victory-Shield-and-how-much-does-it-cost) of $18 per month, $198 per year, or $950 for a lifetime membership, with different in-app purchase prices. Its plans cover unlimited devices for up to ten users, but pricing and platform requirements can change, so verify them before buying.

Covenant Eyes remains the stronger fit when the job is relational accountability across several device types. An alternative should not be called equivalent merely because it blocks adult domains.

## Four questions that identify the right replacement

### 1. Do you want a human to receive activity?

If yes, use a product with a clear reporting mechanism and agree on how the relationship will work. Covenant Eyes highlights flagged and sampled blurred screenshots to an Ally; Canopy's adult human Accountability Partner instead receives requests to approve or deny certain protection changes, according to [Canopy's current accountability documentation](https://support.canopy.us/portal/en/kb/articles/accountability).

SinShield sends no screenshots, browsing history, or activity reports to another person. That is a privacy advantage for a self-directed user, but it means a partner cannot verify activity through SinShield.

### 2. Which devices must be covered?

Covenant Eyes covers the major phone and desktop platforms with platform-specific limits. Canopy supports Android, iPhone and iPad, Mac, Windows, and Chromebook according to its [current device requirements](https://support.canopy.us/portal/en/kb/articles/what-kinds-of-devices-does-canopy-work-with).

SinShield currently supports Android 11 and newer only. If your vulnerable route is an iPhone, Mac, Windows PC, or Chromebook, SinShield cannot replace Covenant Eyes on that device.

### 3. Do you need domains blocked or explicit material filtered inside allowed pages?

Covenant Eyes provides domain filtering and accountability monitoring. Canopy says its Smart Filter analyzes online content in real time, while its own support documentation notes important limits, including that it [does not filter streaming services](https://support.canopy.us/portal/en/kb/articles/how-does-canopy-work-with-streaming-platforms) and does not scan all content inside every non-browser app.

SinShield blocks known adult domains and analyzes visible imagery in supported Android apps, currently Instagram and X. It is intentionally narrower than Canopy's cross-platform web filtering and does not inspect text or video as a general cross-app service.

### 4. How much data are you willing to share?

Accountability requires information to reach another person, while private blocking can operate without that relationship. Read the privacy policy for the exact feature set you enable: Canopy's [privacy policy](https://canopy.us/privacy-policy/) lists account, device, log, installed-app, optional location, and certain content data among the information it may collect to provide services, whereas SinShield requires no account and performs screen-image analysis on the Android device.

Privacy is not automatically the most important criterion. If a trusted person receiving meaningful information is the mechanism you want, a private tool cannot quietly substitute for it.

Canopy is closer when broad device coverage and real-time web-content filtering matter more than screenshot reports. Its official documentation lists custom rules, protection levels, removal prevention, and adult plans with a human partner or AI Ally; the partner approves or denies eligible change requests rather than receiving Covenant Eyes-style screenshot reports.

As of September 24, 2026, Canopy lists one-device, three-device, and up-to-ten-device tiers, with [annual prices from $99.99 to $149.99](https://support.canopy.us/portal/en/kb/articles/how-much-does-canopy-cost-can-i-try-it-for-free) and higher month-to-month prices. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is there a free alternative to Covenant Eyes?

Built-in Apple and Google controls are free, and SinShield is a free Android option. Neither provides Covenant Eyes-style cross-platform screenshot accountability.

### Does SinShield send reports to an accountability partner?

No. SinShield is built around private on-device interruption and does not offer remote activity reports.

### Is Canopy the same as Covenant Eyes?

No. Canopy emphasizes real-time filtering and approval of protected changes, while Covenant Eyes centers screenshot and domain activity shared with an Ally; platform behavior and data flows differ.

### Can Covenant Eyes be bypassed?

No consumer blocker should be treated as impossible to bypass. Covenant Eyes reports some disabling and uninstall activity on supported platforms, but device permissions, platform limits, and determined circumvention remain relevant.

### Which option is most private?

Among the options compared here, SinShield collects the least activity because it requires no account and keeps screen analysis on the Android device. That privacy comes with narrower coverage and no human accountability.

## Where to start

Write one sentence completing this prompt: “I need an alternative because I want more ___ and less ___.” Put human reporting, device coverage, in-page filtering, privacy, and price into those blanks before starting a trial.

If the answer is more private Android protection and less remote monitoring, [install SinShield](/products/android) and test it against your actual routes. If the answer is human accountability across all your devices, keep Covenant Eyes or choose another product that genuinely provides that mechanism rather than settling for a private blocker with a similar label.
