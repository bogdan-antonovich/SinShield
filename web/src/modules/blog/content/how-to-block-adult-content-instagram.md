Adult content can reach you on Instagram even when you did not search for it. The app combines posts from accounts you chose to follow with recommended content in Feed, Reels, Explore, and Search; Meta explains that recommendations can come from accounts you do not follow and become personalized over time according to the [content and accounts you interact with](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/11/introducing-recommendations-reset-instagram/).

That creates several separate routes to an unwanted image. An account you already follow can change what it posts or share somebody else's Reel, Instagram can recommend a post from an unfamiliar account because it predicts that the subject is relevant to you, and public posts can surface through reposts or activity from friends. Content sent in a Direct Message follows another route and is not governed by recommendation preferences.

Instagram removes adult nudity and sexual activity that violate its rules, but some sexually suggestive material is allowed to remain. That is why a post can appear without being a moderation mistake, while another can slip through because it was not recognized or reported. This guide explains which route each control affects in the current Instagram Android app; menu names may move as the app changes.

## What Instagram's adult-content setting actually blocks

Instagram generally removes adult nudity and sexual activity that violate its rules, but it treats some sexually suggestive material differently. A post can be allowed on the platform while still being considered sensitive enough to limit in recommendations.

Meta describes sensitive content as material that may be upsetting without necessarily breaking its rules, including some [sexually suggestive or violent posts](https://about.fb.com/news/2021/07/introducing-sensitive-content-control/). Sensitive Content Control is therefore a recommendation filter, not a promise that no suggestive image will ever appear.

The distinction matters because the **Less** setting mainly changes recommendations from accounts you do not follow. It does not clean up accounts you already follow, inspect every Direct Message, or turn Instagram into a child-safe app.

## How to reduce adult content in Instagram recommendations

### 1. Open Content preferences

From your profile, tap the three-line menu in the upper-right corner.

![Instagram profile with the three-line menu highlighted](/images/inst_1.png "Open your profile and tap the three-line menu.")

On **Settings and activity**, scroll to **What you see** and choose **Content preferences**.

![Instagram Settings and activity screen with Content preferences highlighted](/images/inst_2.png "Open Content preferences from the What you see section.")

If Instagram has rearranged the menu, use the settings search box for **Sensitive content** instead of trying to match every screen exactly.

### 2. Set Sensitive content to Less

Open **Sensitive content**.

![Instagram Content preferences screen with Sensitive content highlighted](/images/inst_3.png "Choose Sensitive content from Content preferences.")

Select **Less**. This asks Instagram to show fewer sensitive recommendations in places such as Explore, Search, Reels, suggested accounts, hashtag pages, and recommendations in your feed.

![Instagram Sensitive content control with Less selected](/images/inst_4.png "Choose Less to reduce sensitive recommendations.")

Do not choose **More** if your goal is to avoid sexual or suggestive material. Teen Accounts are placed into stricter recommendation settings by default, and Meta says its teen protections make potentially sensitive accounts and content harder to find in Search and Explore.

> [!NOTE] What Less cannot do
> Less reduces recommendations; it does not remove every triggering post, affect everything shared by accounts you follow, or prevent someone from sending unwanted content directly.

## Tell Instagram what you do not want to see

When an unwanted suggested post appears, tap its three-dot menu and choose **Not interested**. Instagram should confirm that it will suggest fewer posts like it. Make the choice quickly and leave the post rather than opening the profile or reading its comments.

![Instagram post with its three-dot menu highlighted](/images/inst_10.png "Open the three-dot menu on an unwanted recommendation.")

![Instagram post menu with Not interested highlighted](/images/inst_11.png "Choose Not interested to ask Instagram for fewer similar recommendations.")

This is not an instant block for every similar image. Meta says choosing **Not interested** removes that recommendation and helps Instagram [suggest fewer similar posts in the future](https://about.fb.com/news/2022/08/testing-ways-to-control-what-you-see-instagram/), so repeat the action when the same pattern returns.

For an account you already follow, use the option that fits the situation:

- **Unfollow** it when you no longer want its posts in your feed.
- **Mute** it when you want to stay connected without seeing its posts or Stories.
- **Block** it when you do not want the account to find or contact you.
- **Report** a post when you believe it violates Instagram's rules, rather than merely being an unwanted but permitted recommendation.

## Tighten comments and account privacy when contact is the problem

If sexual comments, spam, or unwanted contact are reaching you through other people, review **Comments**, **Messages and story replies**, **Tags and mentions**, and **Restricted accounts** under **How others can interact with you**.

![Instagram Settings and activity screen with Comments highlighted](/images/inst_5.png "Open Comments under How others can interact with you.")

Inside **Comments**, choose **Posts and reels** under **Who can comment**.

![Instagram Comments settings with Posts and reels highlighted](/images/inst_6.png "Open Posts and reels to choose who may comment.")

Choose the narrowest audience that still fits how you use Instagram. For example, **Your followers** keeps comments available to people following you, while **Off** is the stronger choice when you do not need comments at all.

![Instagram Allow comments from screen with Your followers selected](/images/inst_7.png "Limit comments to followers, mutual connections, or turn them off.")

You can also make the account private. In **Settings and activity**, open **Account privacy** and enable **Private account**. This gives you control over new followers, but it does not filter the recommendations Instagram shows you.

![Instagram Settings and activity screen with Account privacy highlighted](/images/inst_8.png "Open Account privacy under Who can see your content.")

![Instagram Account privacy screen with the Private account control highlighted](/images/inst_9.png "A private account lets you approve new followers.")

These settings solve a different problem from Sensitive Content Control: they reduce who can reach you, while **Less** reduces what Instagram recommends to you.

## For parents and guardians: set up supervision for a teen

> [!NOTE] This section is only for parents and guardians
> Skip this step if you are configuring only your own adult account. Supervision connects a parent's Instagram account to a teen's account so they can manage supported protections together.

Instagram Teen Accounts already include stricter default protections. Parents and guardians who want additional oversight can set up supervision, but both people need Instagram accounts and the teen must approve the connection, as Meta explains in its [parental-supervision guidance](https://about.fb.com/news/2022/03/parental-supervision-tools-instagram-vr/).

From the **parent or guardian's Instagram account**, open **Settings and activity**, then choose **Supervision for Teen Accounts**.

![Instagram Settings and activity screen with Supervision for Teen Accounts highlighted](/images/inst_12.png "Open Supervision for Teen Accounts from the Family Center section.")

Instagram explains what the supervision connection shares before setup begins. Read that screen, tap **Continue**, and send the invitation to the teen; supervision starts only after the teen reviews and approves the request.

![Instagram Supervision for Teen Accounts introduction screen](/images/inst_13.png "Review what supervision provides before starting setup.")

Meta says [Teen Accounts](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/09/instagram-teen-accounts/) use the strictest sensitive-content setting and limit who can contact teens. Those protections are useful, but they still depend on correct account information and Instagram recognizing the material, so they should sit alongside honest conversations and device-level family controls.

## Remove old shortcuts and interaction signals

Filtering future recommendations is only part of the job if unwanted material is still one tap away in your own activity. Review past reposts, saves, likes, and comments that lead back to material you no longer want; this removes those shortcuts and makes your current preferences clearer, but it should not be described as a guaranteed algorithm reset.

### Remove unwanted reposts

Open your profile and choose the **Reposts** tab. Open any Reel or post you no longer want attached to your profile, tap the highlighted repost control, and confirm that you want to undo the repost.

![Instagram profile with the Reposts tab highlighted](/images/inst_14.png "Open the Reposts tab to review content you have reposted.")

![Instagram Reel showing the repost control and repost badge](/images/inst_15.png "Open an unwanted repost and use the repost control to undo it.")

### Clear saved posts you do not want to revisit

From **Settings and activity**, open **Saved**. You can also open **Your activity** from the same screen to review other kinds of past interactions.

![Instagram Settings and activity screen with Saved and Your activity highlighted](/images/inst_16.png "Use Saved for bookmarks and Your activity for earlier interactions.")

In **Saved**, open the post or Reel you want to remove.

![Instagram Saved screen showing saved Reels and posts](/images/inst_17.png "Review the posts and Reels stored in Saved.")

Tap the filled bookmark icon to unsave it. Removing a save closes a private return path; it does not report the post or remove it from Instagram.

![Instagram post with the filled bookmark icon highlighted](/images/inst_18.png "Tap the filled bookmark icon to remove the post from Saved.")

### Review recommendation feedback, likes, and comments

Open **Your activity** to review the posts you marked **Not interested** or **Interested**. Correct an accidental choice here so your explicit recommendation feedback matches what you want now.

![Instagram Your activity screen with Not interested and Interested highlighted](/images/inst_19.png "Review earlier recommendation feedback in Your activity.")

The same area contains **Likes**, **Comments**, and **Reposts**. Meta says [interactions with content help shape recommendations](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/10/improving-your-recommendations-apps-ai-meta/), while **Your activity** lets you manage previous interactions; remove only the engagement you genuinely no longer want associated with your account.

![Instagram Your activity screen with Likes and Comments highlighted](/images/inst_20.png "Review likes, comments, and reposts from Your activity.")

Meta also offers a separate [Reset suggested content](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/11/introducing-recommendations-reset-instagram/) option under **Content preferences** on supported accounts. That reset clears recommendations in Explore, Reels, and Feed so they can personalize again over time; it does not delete your saves, likes, comments, reposts, or followed accounts, so review those separately when they are part of the problem.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Sensitive Content Control block all adult content on Instagram?

No. **Less** reduces sensitive recommendations, especially from accounts you do not follow. It cannot guarantee that every sexual or suggestive image will be removed from the feed, Stories, Reels, profiles, or messages.

### Why do I still see suggestive posts after choosing Less?

The post may come from an account you follow, may not meet Instagram's definition of sensitive content, or may not have been recognized before it reached you. Unfollow or mute repeat sources and use **Not interested** on recommendations.

### Can I put a password on Instagram's Sensitive Content Control?

Instagram does not provide a separate password for that setting on a standard adult account. Teen supervision and Android device controls can add accountability, but none should be described as impossible to bypass.

### Does SinShield work inside Instagram?

On supported Android devices, SinShield can cover explicit or suggestive imagery visible on the screen inside Instagram and other supported apps. It does not change your Instagram account, read private messages as an account service, or send browsing reports to somebody else.

### How do I block adult content on X as well?

X uses a different system because it permits properly labeled adult media. Follow the separate [guide to blocking adult content on X](/blog/how-to-block-adult-content-on-x) for its sensitive-media, safe-search, mute, and autoplay controls.

## Where to start

Start with **Content preferences → Sensitive content → Less**. Then spend a few minutes unfollowing repeat sources and marking unwanted recommendations **Not interested**. Those two changes close the most common routes without forcing you to leave every useful part of Instagram.
